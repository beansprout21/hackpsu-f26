# Ramit Sethi's "I Will Teach You to Be Rich" method: research and design translation for Sprout

Prepared 2026-10-24 for HackPSU Fall 2026 (Sprout, FNB challenge).

**How to read this report.** Sections 1-10 summarize Ramit Sethi's method. Every claim links to the page it came from, and everything is paraphrased; the few short quotes are marked. Sections A-E are our own design analysis for Sprout. They are recommendations, not claims about the method.

**Sources and limits.**
- Ramit's own pages on iwillteachyoutoberich.com (abbreviated IWT below) were preferred. Two of them are the official free Chapter 1 PDF and the 2025 Conscious Spending Plan article.
- Where only secondary coverage existed (GOBankingRates/Nasdaq, Motley Fool, Fortune, book summaries), the report says so.
- No pirated copies of the book were used.

**Assumptions.**
- "The book" means the 2nd edition (Workman, 2019). No 3rd edition was found.
- His 2024 book *Money for Couples* is cited only where it bears on young families.
- Ramit's numbers have changed over time. Where sources disagree, both versions are shown with dates.

## 1. The Rich Life

- **What it is.** A Rich Life is personal: each person decides what "rich" means for them. Money is a tool for living that way on purpose, not a score to maximize. ([Chapter 1 PDF, IWT](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/IWillTeachYoutoBeRich-Chapter1.pdf); [Design Your Rich Life, IWT, 2025](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/design-your-rich-life/))
- **His examples are deliberately ordinary.**
  - Driving the kids to school.
  - Buying groceries without checking prices.
  - A memorable family trip.
  - One luxury item.
  - He stresses that the vision should be the reader's own, not inherited from parents or peers. ([Design Your Rich Life](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/design-your-rich-life/))
- **How he asks people to define it: be specific.**
  - Vague aims like "freedom" or "a million dollars" don't motivate. Concrete scenes do, such as ordering appetizers without looking at prices.
  - A recurring exercise: imagine spending four times as much on your favorite category and describe what that would look like.
  - Sources: [Art of Manliness interview, 2019](https://www.artofmanliness.com/career-wealth/wealth/ramit-sethi-interview-rich-life/); [GOBankingRates, 2023](https://www.gobankingrates.com/money/financial-planning/financial-expert-ramit-sethi-heres-how-to-live-a-rich-life/)
- **How it drives spending.**
  - The core rule: spend generously on what you love and cut costs hard on what you don't. ([Chapter 1 PDF](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/IWillTeachYoutoBeRich-Chapter1.pdf); [Nasdaq/GOBankingRates](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/ramit-sethis-2-big-steps-launch-your-rich-life-one-month))
  - Your brief's wording ("spend extravagantly on what you love, cut costs mercilessly on what you don't") is the widely cited form of this rule. Treat it as his tagline, but quote it only with attribution.
  - He says this is not permission to be reckless: the foundations come first. ([Art of Manliness](https://www.artofmanliness.com/career-wealth/wealth/ramit-sethi-interview-rich-life/); [Money Dials, IWT](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/money-dials/))
  - He also rejects postponing all enjoyment until "later". ([GOBankingRates, 2023](https://www.gobankingrates.com/money/financial-planning/financial-expert-ramit-sethi-heres-how-to-live-a-rich-life/))
- **Sequencing.** His launch advice has two steps: first define the Rich Life, then build the Conscious Spending Plan to fund it. If the Rich Life costs more than you earn, the answer is to raise income (a raise or a side hustle), not to give up the vision. ([Nasdaq/GOBankingRates](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/ramit-sethis-2-big-steps-launch-your-rich-life-one-month))

## 2. The Conscious Spending Plan (CSP)

Take-home pay is split into four buckets. Most budgets focus on what you can't spend; the CSP is meant to fund your priorities first. ([Conscious Spending Basics, IWT](https://iwillteachyoutoberich.com/conscious-spending-basics/); [Conscious Spending, IWT](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/conscious-spending))

| Bucket | % of take-home | What goes in it |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed costs | 50-60% | Rent or mortgage, utilities, insurance, debt payments, groceries, recurring subscriptions. Add a buffer of about 15% on top of this total for price increases and surprises. |
| Investments | ~10% | Long-term retirement accounts (401(k), Roth IRA). Capture any employer match first. |
| Savings | 5-10% | Short-term goals and emergencies. First an emergency fund in a high-yield savings account, then separate pots for goals such as a down payment, vacation, wedding, or gifts. |
| Guilt-free spending | 20-35% | Dining out, clothes, hobbies, entertainment. Spend it without guilt once the other three buckets are funded. |

Where each figure comes from:
- The ranges and bucket definitions: [Conscious Spending Basics](https://iwillteachyoutoberich.com/conscious-spending-basics/); [Nasdaq/GOBankingRates, Oct 2023](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/ramit-sethi:-how-to-create-a-conscious-spending-plan); [Motley Fool](https://fool.com/the-ascent/personal-finance/articles/heres-how-much-of-your-income-should-go-toward-necessities-according-to-ramit-sethi/)
- One summary gives fixed costs as 50-65%. ([Readingraphics](https://readingraphics.com/book-summary-i-will-teach-you-to-be-rich-ramit-sethi/))
- The 15% fixed-cost buffer: [Conscious Spending Basics](https://iwillteachyoutoberich.com/conscious-spending-basics/)
- Sample savings pots (e.g. 2% wedding, 2% down payment, 1% vacation): [Automate Your Finances, IWT](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/automate-your-finances/)
- A GOBankingRates write-up splits discretionary money into a small, unplanned "worry-free" amount (about $50-100/month) and a larger planned guilt-free amount. ([Nasdaq/GOBankingRates](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/ramit-sethi:-how-to-create-a-conscious-spending-plan))

**Adjusting the plan.**
- **The percentages are guidelines.** In high-cost cities, fixed costs can reach 65-70% as long as other buckets give way. Revisit the plan after a raise, a move, a marriage, or a child. ([Conscious Spending Basics](https://iwillteachyoutoberich.com/conscious-spending-basics/))
- **Fixed costs above about 60%: lower them.** Downsize, refinance, or share housing. ([Conscious Spending, IWT](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/conscious-spending))
- **Fixed costs far above 60%: scale everything else down, but keep something in every bucket.** Motley Fool's example: at 80% fixed costs, put 5% to investing, 10% to saving, and 5% to guilt-free spending. Then work over time to cut fixed costs or raise income. ([Motley Fool](https://fool.com/the-ascent/personal-finance/articles/heres-how-much-of-your-income-should-go-toward-necessities-according-to-ramit-sethi/))
- **The opposite failure: oversaving.** If investments plus savings exceed about 20% of take-home, he suggests scaling back so present-day life isn't starved. ([Conscious Spending, IWT](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/conscious-spending))
- **Variable spending: average it.** Estimate each category from three to six months of statements. ([Nasdaq/GOBankingRates](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/ramit-sethi:-how-to-create-a-conscious-spending-plan))

**Where his numbers have shifted.**
- His 2024 "money rules" post sets a higher personal bar: save 10% and invest 20% of gross income. ([10 Money Rules, IWT, 2024](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/money-rules/))
- Treat the CSP ranges as the beginner default and the 2024 figures as a stretch goal.

## 3. Money Dials

- **What they are.** Ten categories people naturally love to spend on. ([Money Dials, IWT](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/money-dials/); [GOBankingRates, 2023](https://www.gobankingrates.com/money/financial-planning/financial-expert-ramit-sethi-heres-how-to-live-a-rich-life/))
  1. Convenience
  2. Travel
  3. Health/fitness
  4. Experiences
  5. Freedom
  6. Relationships
  7. Generosity
  8. Luxury
  9. Social status
  10. Self-improvement
- **The metaphor.** Each dial is like a radio volume knob. Most people have one or two dials they care about. ([The Points Guy, 2019](https://thepointsguy.com/news/turn-your-money-dial-to-travel-talking-points-with-i-will-teach-you-to-be-rich-author-ramit-sethi))
- **How to find yours.**
  - Imagine a $25,000 windfall to spend on one category. The one you pick almost instantly is your main dial.
  - Then check your calendar and card statements to see whether your stated priorities match where your money actually goes.
  - Source: [Money Dials, IWT](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/money-dials/)
- **How to use them.**
  - Turn your main dial up, even all the way up.
  - Turn the others down so you can afford it without guilt.
  - When a purchase spans two dials, decide which one it really serves.
  - Get your foundations in order before splurging.
  - Sources: [Money Dials, IWT](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/money-dials/); [The Points Guy](https://thepointsguy.com/news/turn-your-money-dial-to-travel-talking-points-with-i-will-teach-you-to-be-rich-author-ramit-sethi)
- **Couples.** In *Money for Couples*, each partner identifies their own dials inside a shared spending plan. ([BookTrib, 2024](https://booktrib.com/2024/10/16/money-for-couples-ramit-sethi/))
- **Sprout relevance.** Sprout's swipe deck and persona reveal (for example, "Experience Collector") are essentially a Money Dials quiz. That makes this the cleanest point of inspiration to credit.

## 4. Order of operations: the six-week program

The book is organized as a six-week sequence ([Chapter 1 PDF](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/IWillTeachYoutoBeRich-Chapter1.pdf); [Readingraphics](https://readingraphics.com/book-summary-i-will-teach-you-to-be-rich-ramit-sethi/); [Paul Minors summary](https://paulminors.com/blog/will-teach-you-to-be-rich-book-summary-pdf/?amp=1)):

1. **Week 1: Optimize credit cards.** Pay in full, set up autopay, and get fees waived (details in Section 7).
2. **Week 2: Beat the banks.** Open a no-fee checking account as the "hub" where income lands, plus a separate high-yield savings account.
   - Negotiate away monthly, minimum-balance, and overdraft fees. ([Paul Minors](https://paulminors.com/blog/will-teach-you-to-be-rich-book-summary-pdf/?amp=1))
   - Current IWT checking criteria: no monthly, minimum-balance, or overdraft fees; broad free ATM access; interest if possible; online or branch depending on your needs. ([Best Checking Account, IWT, 2025](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/best-checking-account/))
   - Savings criteria: FDIC-insured, no monthly fees, a competitive yield (he cites about 3.5% or better in 2025), and online banks are fine. He likes the "buckets" feature that splits one account into named goal pots. ([Best Savings Account, IWT, 2025](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/best-savings-account/))
   - When switching banks, keep both accounts open for about a month, re-point autopays, then close the old account with written confirmation. ([Best Checking Account](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/best-checking-account/))
3. **Week 3: Get ready to invest.** He calls this the "Ladder of Personal Finance". ([Nasdaq/GOBankingRates](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/ramit-sethi-6-investing-fundamentals-make-building-wealth-easy); [Paul Minors](https://paulminors.com/blog/will-teach-you-to-be-rich-book-summary-pdf/?amp=1))
   1. Contribute enough to get the full 401(k) employer match.
   2. Pay off high-interest (credit card) debt.
   3. Fund a Roth IRA.
   4. Put more into the 401(k).
   5. Use an HSA if you have one.
   6. Invest anything left in a taxable account.
4. **Week 4: Conscious spending.** Build the CSP (Section 2).
5. **Week 5: Automate.** Set up the money flow (Section 5).
6. **Week 6: Investing.** Low-cost, hands-off investing: a target-date (lifecycle) fund or a few index funds, with no expensive advisor. Check monthly, not daily, and rebalance about once a year. ([Paul Minors](https://paulminors.com/blog/will-teach-you-to-be-rich-book-summary-pdf/?amp=1); [Readingraphics](https://readingraphics.com/book-summary-i-will-teach-you-to-be-rich-ramit-sethi/))

**Ongoing maintenance:** under about an hour a month once the system runs. ([Paul Minors](https://paulminors.com/blog/will-teach-you-to-be-rich-book-summary-pdf/?amp=1))

## 5. Automation: the money flow

**Setup.** About 25 minutes in total ([Automate Your Finances, IWT](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/automate-your-finances/); [Nasdaq/GOBankingRates paycheck routine](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/ramit-sethi-follow-paycheck-routine-get-richer-2025)):
- Link checking to savings, retirement accounts, and the credit card.
- Call companies to move bill due dates so they line up with payday.
- Keep about a $500 buffer in checking while the system beds in.

**Example month (paid on the 1st):**

| Day | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1st | The employer sends part of the paycheck straight to the 401(k). The rest lands in checking, which works as a pass-through hub. |
| ~5th (a few days after payday) | Automatic transfers move money to savings sub-accounts and the Roth IRA, following the CSP percentages. |
| ~7th | Fixed bills autopay, mostly through the credit card. The card is then paid in full from checking. |
| Rest of month | Whatever remains in checking is guilt-free spending. |

**Starting small.** If 5-10% savings isn't affordable, start with a token amount such as $5 just to prove the system works. ([Automate Your Finances](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/automate-your-finances/))

**Other pay schedules.**
- **Paid twice a month:** mirror the flow on the 1st and 15th. One version uses the first check for bills and the second for saving and investing. ([Nasdaq/GOBankingRates](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/ramit-sethi-follow-paycheck-routine-get-richer-2025))
- **Windfalls** (tax refunds, bonuses): split between fun and long-term goals; 50/50 is one option. ([Nasdaq/GOBankingRates](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/ramit-sethi-follow-paycheck-routine-get-richer-2025))
- **Irregular income:** see Section 8.

**Day-to-day monitoring.** Track only the one or two categories you most want to control, and check mid-month. ([Automate Your Finances](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/automate-your-finances/))

## 6. Big Wins vs. small savings; negotiation

- **Big Wins.** A few large decisions matter far more than daily purchases ([Chapter 1 PDF](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/IWillTeachYoutoBeRich-Chapter1.pdf); [Art of Manliness](https://www.artofmanliness.com/career-wealth/wealth/ramit-sethi-interview-rich-life/)):
  - automating savings and investing
  - negotiating salary
  - investing consistently
  - housing and car decisions
  - steadily raising your savings rate
- **The latte question.** He's said all he wants to say about lattes: people who get the Big Wins right don't need to sweat small purchases. His framing is that most people ask "$3 questions" when they should be asking "$30,000 questions". ([Fortune, July 2019](https://fortune.com/2019/07/05/ramit-sethi-updates-i-will-teach-you-to-be-rich-but-some-advice-stays-the-same-buy-the-latte))
- **The 85% Solution.** Getting roughly 85% of the way, and starting now, beats waiting for a perfect plan. ([GOBankingRates, 2023](https://www.gobankingrates.com/money/financial-planning/financial-expert-ramit-sethi-heres-how-to-live-a-rich-life/); [Develop Good Habits summary](https://www.developgoodhabits.com/teach-rich-summary-kw1/))
- **Negotiation, at a high level.**
  - Call card issuers and banks to ask that late fees, annual fees, and monthly account fees be waived. Reliable customers can also ask for a lower APR. ([Chapter 1 PDF](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/IWillTeachYoutoBeRich-Chapter1.pdf))
  - He says the late-fee call usually works, and that it teaches people that companies can be negotiated with. ([Tim Ferriss Show transcript, 2019](https://tim.blog/2019/05/23/the-tim-ferriss-show-transcripts-ramit-sethi-automating-finances-negotiating-prenups-disagreeing-with-tim-and-more-371/))
  - **Pattern of his scripts:**
    1. State that you're a good customer.
    2. Name the specific ask.
    3. Cite competing offers where relevant.
    4. Escalate to a supervisor if the first answer is no.
    - Sources: [6 Telephone Scripts sales page, IWT](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/special/6-powerful-telephone-scripts/); [Pay Off Credit Card Debt, IWT](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/pay-off-credit-card-debt)
  - Readers report waived late, overdraft, and monthly fees and lower phone and internet bills. These are testimonials, not measured success rates. ([6 Telephone Scripts](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/special/6-powerful-telephone-scripts/))
- **Earning more** is the other half of Big Wins: salary negotiation and side income. ([Develop Good Habits](https://www.developgoodhabits.com/teach-rich-summary-kw1/); [Yahoo/GOBankingRates, 2025](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/ramit-sethi-top-4-investing-161135475.html))

## 7. Emergency fund, debt, credit

### Emergency fund

His target has moved over time:

| Source | Target |
|---|---|
| CSP pages | 3-6 months of living expenses in high-yield savings ([Conscious Spending Basics](https://iwillteachyoutoberich.com/conscious-spending-basics/)) |
| Debt-payoff guide (starting point) | $500-1,000 first, automated in small transfers ([Pay Off Credit Card Debt](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/pay-off-credit-card-debt)) |
| Since about 2021 | His own ideal is 12 months; 6 months is acceptable for people who want less ([Motley Fool, 2021](https://www.fool.com/money/banks/articles/heres-how-much-ramit-sethi-says-you-should-have-in-emergency-savings); [10 Money Rules, 2024](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/money-rules/)) |

Why it matters to him: a cushion is what lets people make bolder moves later. ([Motley Fool, 2021](https://www.fool.com/money/banks/articles/heres-how-much-ramit-sethi-says-you-should-have-in-emergency-savings))

**Recommendation:** use the tiers $500-1,000, then 3 months, then 6+ months.

### Debt payoff

IWT step-by-step guide ([Pay Off Credit Card Debt](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/pay-off-credit-card-debt)):
1. List every debt with its APR and minimum payment.
2. Choose avalanche (highest APR first) or snowball (smallest balance first), and spend no more than about five minutes deciding.
3. Call each issuer to negotiate a lower APR.
4. Treat balance transfers with caution: they often hide the overspending problem.
5. Automate at least the minimums, then add extra on payday.
6. Change the habits that created the debt.
7. Raise income.
8. Build a starter emergency fund so the next surprise doesn't land back on the card.
9. If you're overwhelmed, use nonprofit credit counseling (NFCC, MMI) and avoid debt-settlement firms with upfront fees.

**Student loans** ([GOBankingRates, 2025](https://www.gobankingrates.com/loans/student/reasons-not-to-pay-off-your-student-loans-early-according-to-ramit-sethi/)):
- Low-rate federal loans (about 4-6%) are usually not worth prepaying before the emergency fund and credit card debt are handled.
- Higher-rate private loans make a stronger case for paying early.
- Refinancing federal loans can forfeit income-driven repayment and forgiveness. ([Pay Off Credit Card Debt](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/pay-off-credit-card-debt))

### Credit score and credit cards (Chapter 1)

Source for this subsection: [Chapter 1 PDF](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/IWillTeachYoutoBeRich-Chapter1.pdf).

**What makes up a FICO score:**

| Factor | Weight |
|---|---|
| Payment history | ~35% |
| Amounts owed (utilization) | ~30% |
| Length of history | ~15% |
| New credit | ~10% |
| Credit mix | ~10% |

**His credit card rules:**
1. Always pay on time, ideally in full on autopay.
2. Ask for fee waivers and a lower APR.
3. Keep old cards open and active, for example with one small autopaid subscription.
4. Keep the number of cards manageable, about two or three.
5. Raise your limit to lower utilization, but only if you carry no debt.
6. If you miss a payment, call right away. A late fee can often be reversed, and a payment a few days late usually isn't reported.

**Other points:**
- Prefer simple cash-back cards. Avoid store cards and mailed offers.
- His cost examples: minimum payments on a $1,000 phone take about nine years and cost about $730 in interest. Credit score tiers change the total cost of a 30-year $200k mortgage by about $70k.
- The chapter's framing: early, consistent planners do better than reactors. In his words, "rich people plan before they need to plan."
- Points chasing: he warns that most people waste time on miles. Experts agree rewards only pay off if you pay in full. ([GOBankingRates, 2025](https://www.gobankingrates.com/money/ramit-sethis-financial-hot-takes-do-experts-agree/))

## 8. Starting out, low income, irregular income

- **Irregular income** ([Automate Your Finances](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/automate-your-finances/); [Nasdaq/GOBankingRates](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/ramit-sethi-follow-paycheck-routine-get-richer-2025)):
  1. Work out your bare-bones monthly survival cost (rent, utilities, food, loan minimums).
  2. Before investing, build a buffer of three months of that cost in a savings sub-account. Fund it by redirecting the money you'd have invested and by banking extra income from good months.
  3. Once the buffer is full, run the normal plan as if you had a steady paycheck. His example: $1,500/month bare bones means a $4,500 buffer.
- **Low income** (his $25K example: [Yahoo/GOBankingRates, Oct 2025](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/ramit-sethi-top-4-investing-161135475.html)):
  - He admits the budget is tight, and argues that is exactly when habits matter most.
  - Build the emergency fund first.
  - Use the CSP ranges, but temporarily lower the targets when housing is high.
  - Start investing with small amounts (his example is $20/month) because starting early beats starting big.
  - Raise income through skills and side gigs; tutoring and freelancing are on his list.
- **Starting early** is the book's opening argument: a person who invests for 10 years starting at 35 ends up ahead of one who invests for 20 years starting at 45. ([Chapter 1 PDF](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/IWillTeachYoutoBeRich-Chapter1.pdf))
- **Target reader and students.**
  - Summaries describe the book as aimed at 20- and 30-somethings in the US. ([Readingraphics](https://readingraphics.com/book-summary-i-will-teach-you-to-be-rich-ramit-sethi/))
  - Cal Newport's review frames it for new graduates. His summary of the priorities: no-fee checking plus high-interest savings, the right number of cards, 401(k) to the match then a Roth IRA, a deliberate spending plan instead of restriction, and automation. ([Cal Newport, 2009](https://calnewport.com/how-to-go-from-college-to-an-extraordinary-life-and-bank-account/))
- **Young families.** *Money for Couples* (Workman, Dec 2024) adds ([BookTrib](https://booktrib.com/2024/10/16/money-for-couples-ramit-sethi/)):
  - a shared Rich Life vision
  - scripts for a first money talk and a monthly check-in
  - a couples CSP built on four key numbers
  - each partner's own Money Dials
  - teaching kids about money

## 9. Tone and communication principles

All paraphrased.

**Overall register.**
- Blunt, funny, and impatient with excuses, media hype, and finance jargon. ([Chapter 1 PDF](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/IWillTeachYoutoBeRich-Chapter1.pdf))
- Encouraging rather than scolding: candid about banks and "experts", warm toward the reader. ([Art of Manliness](https://www.artofmanliness.com/career-wealth/wealth/ramit-sethi-interview-rich-life/))
- His own pages present budgets as punitive and his plan as permission. ([Conscious Spending, IWT](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/conscious-spending))

**What makes it logical.**
- Every rule has a number and an order (percent ranges, a ladder, days of the month).
- He shows the math: compounding examples, interest on minimum payments.
- Sources: [Chapter 1 PDF](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/IWillTeachYoutoBeRich-Chapter1.pdf); [Automate Your Finances](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/automate-your-finances/)

**What makes it optimistic.**
- Spending on joy is built into the plan.
- Starting small counts.
- The vision is the user's own.
- Sources: [Money Dials](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/money-dials/); [Yahoo/GOBankingRates](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/ramit-sethi-top-4-investing-161135475.html)

**What makes it simple.**
- Four buckets, one automated flow, and about an hour a month of upkeep.
- 85% is good enough.
- Sources: [Paul Minors](https://paulminors.com/blog/will-teach-you-to-be-rich-book-summary-pdf/?amp=1); [GOBankingRates, 2023](https://www.gobankingrates.com/money/financial-planning/financial-expert-ramit-sethi-heres-how-to-live-a-rich-life/)

## 10. Criticisms and limitations

1. **The title overpromises.** Better spending and a modest raise don't by themselves close the gap to real wealth. The advice is useful but not transformative for people who already manage money carefully. ([Tom MacWright review, 2021](https://macwright.com/2021/06/30/teach-you-to-be-rich))
2. **It assumes someone earning a salary.** The CSP percentages, the 401(k) match, and payday automation all presume steady take-home pay and an employer plan. Low and irregular income get a side note: the three-month buffer, and admitting that $25K is tight. ([Automate Your Finances](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/automate-your-finances/); [Yahoo/GOBankingRates](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/ramit-sethi-top-4-investing-161135475.html))
3. **It's built for the US.** 401(k), Roth IRA, and HSA have no direct equivalents abroad. ([Readingraphics](https://readingraphics.com/book-summary-i-will-teach-you-to-be-rich-ramit-sethi/))
4. **His numbers have moved.**
   - Emergency fund: 3-6 months, then 12 months.
   - Investing: 10% of take-home in the CSP, then 20% of gross in his 2024 rules.
   - Some IWT pages contain internal arithmetic mismatches.
   - An app should pin one version and say which.
   - Sources: [Motley Fool, 2021](https://www.fool.com/money/banks/articles/heres-how-much-ramit-sethi-says-you-should-have-in-emergency-savings); [10 Money Rules](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/money-rules/); [Conscious Spending Basics](https://iwillteachyoutoberich.com/conscious-spending-basics/)
5. **Experts dispute some hot takes.** One example is whether chasing an extra 0.15% of interest is a waste of time. On "you don't need to be debt-free to invest", experts add that it depends on the interest rate. ([GOBankingRates, Nov 2025](https://www.gobankingrates.com/money/ramit-sethis-financial-hot-takes-do-experts-agree/))
6. **Renting vs. buying.** His pro-renting stance is publicly debated. ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/renting-better-buying-ramit-sethis-controversial-take-wealth); [Fortune, 2019](https://fortune.com/2019/07/05/ramit-sethi-updates-i-will-teach-you-to-be-rich-but-some-advice-stays-the-same-buy-the-latte))
7. **Commercial funnel.** IWT pages promote paid programs ($30-49/month memberships, script ebooks), and the negotiation success claims are testimonials. ([IWT 2nd edition page](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/i-will-teach-you-to-be-rich-second-edition); [6 Telephone Scripts](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/special/6-powerful-telephone-scripts/))
8. **Guilt-free spending can drift.** His own case studies include couples overspending the bucket by about $1,000 a month. ([Conscious Spending Basics](https://iwillteachyoutoberich.com/conscious-spending-basics/))

# Design translation for Sprout (our analysis)

Context checked against `/home/claude/hackpsu-f26/docs/SPROUT.md` (engine rule sets A-E) and `docs/SPEC.md`.

## A. What maps cleanly for a student with irregular or no income

**Maps directly:**

| IWT idea | Sprout equivalent |
|---|---|
| Rich Life + Money Dials | Swipe deck → persona → goals. Already Sprout's opening flow. |
| Four buckets with guilt-free spending protected | Rule set B already protects guilt-free spending. |
| Automate on the day money arrives | Rule set C step 2 is exactly this. |
| Beat the banks: no-fee checking as the hub, separate savings with goal pots | Step one: open FNB Student Checking; Goal CD / savings next. |
| Starter emergency fund, then grow it | Starter cushion (1× fixed costs), then full cushion (3×). |
| Credit card rules (pay in full, autopay, few cards, ask for waivers) | Secured-card unlock in rule set D, plus the copy that goes with it. |
| The 85% Solution / start with $5 | "Optimism that leads to action" theme; a small surplus is framed as a start. |

**Adapting percentages when income is near zero.** Percentages of a near-zero number are meaningless, so the IWT order matters more than its ratios.

1. **Smooth the income first.** Rule set A's effective monthly income does what Ramit's irregular-income advice does: it averages lumpy money into a monthly figure.
2. **Fund fixed costs at their actual amount.** Don't force 50-60%. Ramit himself allows 65-70% and scales the other buckets down.
3. **Split the surplus, not the income.** This is his "keep something in every bucket" adjustment for high fixed costs.
4. **While the starter cushion is unfunded, the investing share goes to the cushion.** This follows his irregular-income rule directly.
5. **Surplus ≤ 0: no percentages at all.** The plan becomes "track one month" plus fee avoidance, a Big Win that costs nothing. This matches rule set B's breathing-room path.

**Check: Maya (golden test).**
- Fixed costs are 77% of effective income (650 / 841.67).
- The current engine gives about $58 emergency (6.9%), $77 goals (9.1%), and $58 guilt-free (6.9%).
- That is close to Motley Fool's IWT example for 80% fixed costs (5% invest / 10% save / 5% guilt-free).
- Conclusion: the engine already behaves like the IWT high-fixed-cost adjustment. Good to cite.

## B. Representing the investing bucket honestly within the no-investment-advice boundary

The goal is to keep the method's shape without giving investment advice (rule set E).

1. **Show a fourth "Future You" row, never an invisible gap.**
   - Name it "Future You (long-term)".
   - Status "Not yet" with an unlock condition, which reuses the `later` pattern: "Unlocks when your cushion is full and you have steady income."
2. **Allowed copy is attribution only.**
   - Allowed: "The plan Sprout is inspired by sets aside about 10% for long-term goals once your safety cushion is built. Sprout doesn't give investing advice. An FNB advisor can help when you're ready."
   - Not allowed: account types, funds, allocations, or "you should invest $X".
3. **For students, $0 goes to Future You, and the copy says why.** The method itself sends would-be investing money to the buffer first when income is irregular. Sprout is following the method, not watering it down.
4. **For young families, show the slice but don't fill it.**
   - Display a suggested amount (about 10%) as earmarked "for long-term goals". The action is "Talk to an FNB advisor", not a product.
   - If the lead decides even a number is too close to advice, show the row as "Set aside with an advisor" with no amount.
   - **This is a spec/contract decision for the lead agent; do not implement without it.**
5. **Never relabel a CD or savings product as investing.** Goal CDs stay in goal savings. Mislabelling would be a credibility hit with FNB judges.
6. **Honest framing for the pitch:** "We cover the parts of the method a bank can responsibly automate (spending, saving, credit) and hand off the part that needs a licensed advisor."

## C. Recommended bucket models

Percentages are of effective monthly take-home. They are written so they can run on the existing surplus-split engine plus one guard.

### C1. Student, irregular income (Maya-like)

| Bucket | Target | Justification against IWT |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed costs | Actual amount. Flag if > 60%; no shame copy below 80%. | CSP is 50-60%; 65-70% is allowed when other buckets give way. |
| Safety cushion | 30% of surplus until 1× fixed + debt is saved (starter). Then the share moves to goals. Stretch target: 3× bare-bones. | $500-1,000 starter, then a 3-month buffer for irregular earners before investing. |
| Goal savings | 40% of surplus (70% once the starter is met), across up to 3 goals. | 5-10% savings pots for specific goals. Pots like his 2% / 2% / 1% examples. |
| Guilt-free | 30% of surplus, never zero when surplus > 0. | 20-35% bucket. "Keep something in every bucket" when fixed costs are high. |
| Future You | 0%, "unlocks when" (see B). | Irregular earners fill the buffer before investing. |
| Windfalls (aid refund, summer pay) | Spread across 9 months (rule set A). Suggest letting a one-time surplus top up the cushion first. | Bank extra from good months into the buffer; split windfalls between fun and goals. |

**Optional guard for students with very low fixed costs** (e.g. living at home, fixed costs < 40%):
- Problem: 70% of the surplus going to savings can exceed IWT's "about 20% combined" ceiling.
- Recommendation: allow it while the cushion is unfunded, which IWT supports for irregular earners.
- After the cushion is met, cap combined savings at about 20-25% of income and send the rest to guilt-free spending.

### C2. Young family, steady income (Jordan-like)

| Bucket | Target | Justification against IWT |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed costs | 50-60%, flag above 60%; include a 15% fixed-cost buffer in the display | CSP ranges and the fixed-cost buffer |
| Future You | About 10% shown as earmarked, with an advisor handoff (see B) | CSP investments of about 10% |
| Safety cushion + goals | 10% combined: cushion first to 3 months (stretch: 6), then a home down payment pot (20% down is his rule) and other goals | CSP savings 5-10%; 3-6 months (12 is his personal ideal); 20% down |
| Guilt-free | 20-25% default (whatever remains, floor 20%) | CSP 20-35% |

**Engine gap to escalate (do not change the engine on our own).**
- At 55% fixed costs, the current 30/40/30 split of the surplus gives about 13.5% guilt-free and about 31.5% saved.
- Against IWT, that is below the 20% guilt-free floor and above the roughly 20% combined savings ceiling.
- Proposed rule: guilt-free = max(30% of surplus, 20% of income) when fixed costs ≤ 60%, and the excess is taken from goal savings.
- This needs a `SPROUT.md` / contract decision from the lead agent.

## D. Tone rules for the Sprout advisor (derived, paraphrased)

1. **Start from the person's vision, then the math.**
   - Good: "Japan next summer is your big one. Here's how $77 a month gets you there."
   - Bad: "Your discretionary spending is too high relative to income."
2. **Permission, not restriction.** Guilt-free spending is presented as planned.
   - Good: "$58 a month is yours to enjoy, no tracking needed."
   - Bad: "Try to limit fun spending to $58."
3. **Big Wins over lattes.** Never nag about small purchases; point to the one move that matters.
   - Good: "The biggest win this month is a no-fee checking account. It saves more than skipping coffee would."
   - Bad: "Cutting your daily coffee could save you $90 a month."
4. **Specific numbers and an order.** Every recommendation gets an amount, a date, and a reason, in that order.
   - Good: "On the day your refund lands, move $58 to your cushion. That's step one."
   - Bad: "Consider saving more when you can."
5. **Small starts count (85%).**
   - Good: "Even $5 a week proves the system works. We'll raise it after your first paycheck."
   - Bad: "You need at least $500 before this is worth doing."
6. **"Not yet" means "unlocks when".**
   - Good: "A secured card unlocks once your cushion reaches $1,150, or with your first paycheck."
   - Bad: "You don't qualify for a credit card."
7. **Confident, plain, and a little warm. No jargon, no guru swagger.** Borrow his clarity, not his provocations. Sprout speaks for a bank brand to students, so drop the in-your-face edge.
   - Good: "Pay the card in full every month on autopay. That one habit does most of the work for your credit."
   - Bad: "Stop making excuses and fix your credit."
8. **Never shame, never moralize identity.** No "bad with money".
   - Good: "Your costs take most of your money right now. That's common in college, so we'll start by finding breathing room."
   - Bad: "You're overspending."
9. **Be honest about the boundary.**
   - Good: "Long-term investing is part of the plan we're inspired by. An FNB advisor can help with that when you're ready."
   - Bad: "Put 10% in an index fund."
